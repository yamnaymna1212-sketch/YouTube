## Description (long, with full source links)
The U.S. national debt passed $40 trillion for the first time on August 18, 2026, and it's already more than $40.5 trillion. This year the government paid about $1.05 trillion in net interest: roughly $2.9 billion a day, $33,000 a second, more than it spent on national defense.

So what does that do to you? We follow the money through three doors: your taxes and services, your loans (a $350,000 mortgage costs about $223 more a month than a year ago) and the dollar itself. And we check the "dollar collapse" headlines against the evidence: the dollar is still about 57% of world reserves, foreign holdings of U.S. debt are roughly flat, and the Fed's dollar index is about where it was a year ago.

Both sides of the debate are presented. CBO numbers are projections under current law, not predictions. Educational only, not financial advice.
